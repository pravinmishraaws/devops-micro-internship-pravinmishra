# Assignment 1. What is something you believe to be true that most people around you would disagree with?

### Rules

* No "safe" answers.
* Must be your real belief (not copied from internet).
* Minimum 50 words.

**Hint:** What do you believe about career, money, learning, discipline, relationships, health, success, life, tech industry, etc. that most people don't agree with?

## Answer

**I believe that rejection is not always a reflection of one's ability.**

Many people around me see rejection as proof that they are not good enough or that they should give up on a particular goal. I understand why they feel that way because rejection can be deeply personal. It has the ability to shake a person's confidence, create self-doubt, and make them question their worth or potential. Whether it comes from a job application, an interview, or any important opportunity, rejection often leaves people wondering if they were ever good enough in the first place. When it is not handled well, rejection can cause people to abandon dreams, walk away from promising careers, or lose the determination to build something meaningful with their lives.

However, I have come to believe that rejection is not always a reflection of one's ability. In many situations, it reflects factors beyond an individual's capability, such as timing, organizational priorities, competition, or simply being a better fit for another opportunity. While there are times when rejection highlights areas that require improvement, it should not be treated as a final verdict on a person's potential.

This belief has shaped the way I approach setbacks. Rather than allowing rejection to define me, I choose to examine it objectively. I ask myself what I can learn from the experience, what skills I can improve, and how I can prepare better for the next opportunity. This approach allows me to grow instead of becoming discouraged.

I also believe that one of the greatest dangers of rejection is not the disappointment itself, but the possibility of allowing it to become the end of the journey. When people stop trying because they have been rejected, they also close the door to future opportunities that they may have been fully capable of achieving. Growth often requires persistence, and persistence is only possible when we refuse to let one setback determine our future.

This mindset has helped me remain focused on my long-term goals, even when progress has been slower than I expected. It has reminded me that success is rarely defined by never experiencing rejection, but by continuing to learn, improve, and move forward despite it. Rejection may shape our journey, but it should never define our ability or our future potential.

# Reflection – Week 1

### Biggest insight I got about myself this week
The biggest insight I gained this week is that I function best when I have structure and clarity guiding my actions. When I take time to organize my thoughts, define my goals, and break them into smaller intentional steps, I am able to focus better and execute more effectively.

I also realized that I have the ability to stay organized and deliver results even when working under pressure or within a short amount of time. This week showed me that even in busy or demanding situations, I can still maintain clarity, prioritize effectively, and complete tasks without losing focus.

At the same time, I noticed that I tend to carry multiple thoughts, responsibilities, and goals at once, which can create mental noise. However, once I intentionally write things down and structure them, I feel more in control and less overwhelmed.

This week reinforced that discipline is not only about working hard, but also about creating systems that make consistency easier to maintain.

### My biggest weakness/loop I noticed

My biggest weakness is that I sometimes tend to overthink and mentally carry multiple tasks, responsibilities, and goals at the same time without immediately breaking them down into actionable steps. This can create unnecessary mental pressure and slow down my ability to start tasks quickly.

I also noticed a pattern where I sometimes try to organize everything in my head before taking action, instead of writing things down or structuring them early enough. This can lead to delays in execution even when I already know what needs to be done.

In addition, my quest for perfection can sometimes make what should have been a simple or straightforward task become unnecessarily complicated. Instead of focusing on progress, I may spend extra time trying to make things overly refined, which affects speed and efficiency.

However, this week helped me recognize that when I intentionally document my thoughts and create structure, I become more focused, efficient, and less overwhelmed. I am learning to shift from thinking about tasks to actively organizing and executing them in a more structured way.

### One system I will implement from this week (exact habit + time)

I will implement a daily deep work habit focused on DevOps learning and hands-on practice. I will dedicate 1–2 hours every weekday between 7:00 PM and 9:00 PM to structured learning, labs, and completing internship-related tasks.

During this time, I will avoid all distractions and focus only on one clear learning or execution goal per session, such as completing a lab, practicing a tool, or documenting what I learned.

This system is designed to build consistency, improve my technical skills, and ensure steady progress toward becoming a DevOps Engineer.
